We fitted a small vector for each of 100 held-out respondents using their existing answers, then predicted responses to three reserved questions and five new policy scenarios.

1. What physically exists?

final_personas.json contains all 100 vectors and the evidence used to fit them. novel_policy_responses.json contains 500 sampled answers with their full option probabilities. The personas are learned representations of real panel members; no fictional biographies were invented.

Qwen supplies fixed semantic features. Only eight numbers per new person were fitted. The shared model, PCA, 200 fitting steps and regularization were frozen before final evaluation.

2. Accuracy on real, unseen answers

Distribution error is mean total variation distance. Support error is mean absolute error in the combined support share. Lower is better for both.

The persona model differs from the population baseline by +14.0 percentage points in accuracy and -0.130 in log loss. We report the result without changing or reselecting the model. Aggregate distribution error remains similar to the population baseline, and the shared-only model has lower support-share error. The improvement is in predicting individual answers; it does not establish better aggregate policy forecasts.

Paired respondent-bootstrap 95% interval for the accuracy difference: +7.3 to +20.7 percentage points; for log loss: -0.222 to -0.034. These describe people on three fixed questions, not generalization to arbitrary policies or uncertainty from model selection.

3. The decision and five new questions

Decision: which budget allocations should advance to a real human pilot survey?

Consider a hypothetical federal program for 2027 with a fixed total budget of $1 billion, including delivery costs, to help low-income U.S. households with home energy costs. The money has already been set aside; choosing a package does not change total spending or taxes. Bill credits reduce recipients' electricity or heating bills during 2027. Home energy improvements, such as insulation and efficient heating equipment, are intended to reduce bills in later years. Both renters and homeowners can qualify; support for rental properties must benefit the tenant. The five packages are alternatives, and only one would be implemented. Evaluate each package independently.

For each package, ask: Would you support or oppose...

  1. 100% credits / 0% improvements

    Allocating 100% of the program budget to immediate bill credits and 0% to home energy improvements?

  2. 75% credits / 25% improvements

    Allocating 75% of the program budget to immediate bill credits and 25% to home energy improvements?

  3. 50% credits / 50% improvements

    Allocating 50% of the program budget to immediate bill credits and 50% to home energy improvements?

  4. 25% credits / 75% improvements

    Allocating 25% of the program budget to immediate bill credits and 75% to home energy improvements?

  5. 0% credits / 100% improvements

    Allocating 0% of the program budget to immediate bill credits and 100% to home energy improvements?

Single-select options: Strongly oppose · Somewhat oppose · Neither oppose nor support · Somewhat support · Strongly support.

Simulation only. There are no human answers to these five new questions. The predicted support range is 61.1%–61.9%, a 0.7 percentage-point spread. The numerically highest package is 25% credits / 75% improvements; this does not establish a winning policy. The model barely distinguishes the allocations; differences are smaller than its measured support-share error on the reserved questions, although that error is not a confidence interval for this new survey.

Use these outputs to formulate hypotheses and design a pilot. Approval of several packages is possible; the survey does not measure exclusive preference between them. Headline shares average probabilities. The 500 sampled answers are one reproducible draw, not additional evidence.

5. What this supports

A complete, reproducible demonstration: real evidence → 100 learned representations → new-question predictions → a frozen human-answer evaluation. Personalization had mixed validation results, so the common population prediction remains an essential comparator.

For a consequential decision, collect real answers to these five packages and test whether the model correctly predicts differences between them. This historical U.S. Prolific panel is not population weighted and does not establish 2027 public opinion. Related policy questions share an instrument; public-model pretraining contamination cannot be ruled out.
